Dr Defrance is a research scientist specialised in superconducting technologies for quantum computing and quantum sensing, and in metamaterial optics for terahertz (THz) frequencies. He obtained his Ph.D. from Sorbonne University in 2015, after which he joined the California Institute of Technology (Caltech) in 2017 to develop NEW-MUSIC, a ground-based astronomical instrument designed to observe the cosmic microwave background and galaxy clusters using superconducting kinetic inductance detectors (KIDs). During five years at Caltech, first as a postdoctoral researcher and later as a research scientist, he worked on superconducting KID development (design, modelling, and testing) and on low-loss silicon metamaterial optics for THz frequencies.

In 2022, he obtained a permanent position as microwave engineer at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL), working on microwave and sub-millimeter instruments for NASA space missions while continuing his research on KIDs with Caltech. At JPL, he designed a microwave radiometer front-end for earth tropical storm observation (the INCUS satellite mission) and created a novel membrane-based superconducting filter-bank for 450–600 GHz.

Since November 2025, he has joined the Catalan Institute of Nanoscience and Nanotechnology (ICN2) in Spain, applying his experience in superconducting resonators to develop a superconducting quantum transducer capable of converting microwave photons into optical photons — a key enabling technology for distributed quantum computer architectures.
